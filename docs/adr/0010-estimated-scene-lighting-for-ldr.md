# ADR 0010: Estimated scene lighting for JPG/PNG and bundled scenes

## Status

Accepted. Contradicts ADR 0009's "only HDR images carry the light" on purpose. The brightness constants and the
in-game look are UNVERIFIED until the in-game checks are done.

## Context

Scene lighting (ADR 0009) reads real radiance from HDR images. JPG/PNG images and the 8 bundled scenes, which are
JPEG-derived and shipped as pre-converted `.atex` (ADR 0005), showed a note and got no lights. Users asked for lights
there too, including from lamps in interior scenes. An 8-bit image clips every bright source at 255, so the real energy
is lost. The direction can still be recovered from where the clipped pixels sit, and the colour from the unclipped glow
around them. The brightness has to be assumed.

## Decision

- **One pure estimator** (`Milieu/Lighting/LdrLightEstimator.cs`). `RadianceProbe.BuildSrgb8` makes one pass over the
  8-bit rows. It produces the linear 256x128 probe (the same accumulation as `Build`, pinned by a SHA golden) and a
  2048x1024 `ClipMask` (cells of 0.176°, the fraction of clipped source pixels).
- **Clip rule** (`LdrLightEstimator.IsClipped`, the single definition): a pixel is clipped when min(R,G,B) ≥ 245
  (white), or when R ≥ 250, G ≥ 250 and B ≥ 160 (a bright yellow core).
  - Pale sky, sand, skin and orange signage are not clipped.
  - A cream wall in full sun is clipped, and a warm lamp core with G under 250 is missed (known limits).
- **Labelling:** hysteresis on the mask, seeding at a fraction ≥ 0.25 and growing through 8-connected cells ≥ 0.1. It
  wraps horizontally but not at the poles. Each component gets Ω (fraction-weighted solid angle), a centroid and a
  radius.
- **Filters, in order:**
  - (a) Speck: Ω < 1e-5 sr.
  - (b) Broad: radius > 12°.
  - (c) Saturated image: more than 25% of the covered sphere is near-white clipped (the warm branch never counts), so
    no point sources at all.
  - (d) Share: Ω < 5% of the largest point blob.
  - (e) Outlier: with more than 8 point blobs (those passing (a)–(b)), a blob must have Ω ≥ 3× the median point blob.
    - Fallback: if (e) would reject every blob passing (d), and at most 32 did (`FallbackMaxBlobs`, ND-5), (e) is
      skipped. A room of similar downlights keeps its lamps; a star field with hundreds of stars gets fills only.
  - (f) Glow: the halo's mean Y must be ≥ 1.25× the covered median Y. A zero median or an empty ring passes.
- **Ranking and lights:** survivors are ranked by Ω (ties go to the lower first member cell in row-major order).
  - Rank 0 is the key; the next up to 3 are lamps.
  - Each is coloured by its halo, the ring (radius, radius + 3°]: luminance-weighted, scaled to luminance 1, saturation
    capped at 0.5 toward white.
  - Each is weighted by Ω/Ω_key.
  - The key's and lamps' probe texels are zeroed, and the rest is median-cut into the four fill regions
    (`LightExtractor.MedianCut`). `TotalEnergy` is the regions' sum.
- **Brightness at plan time only** (`LightRigPlanner.Resolve`):
  - k = `EstimatedKeyOverSky` (2) × max(TotalEnergy_Y, `EstimatedSkyFloorY` = 0.05·4π). The key and lamps are scaled
    by k and added to the total.
  - Lamps take fill slots before regions, at `LampPower` (30).
  - Tuning these constants invalidates no cache or table.
- **Rig model and cache:** `LightRig` gains `Lamps` and `EstimatorVersion`, and its JSON gains `"lamps"` and
  `"estimated"`, written only for estimated rigs.
  - `LightRigStore.Parse/Load` check the flag both ways, so an HDR rig is never used as an estimate or the other way
    round.
  - Estimated rigs use `CacheStore.EstimatedRigKey`.
  - A JPG/PNG conversion stores its estimate (gated on the lighting kill switch, never failing the conversion), and
    `PrepareLightsAsync` estimates on a cache miss.
- **Bundled scenes: an embedded table.** `Milieu/Assets/BundledLightRigs.Data.cs` (data only) is generated by
  `PrepareBackdrops rigs Milieu/DefaultBackdrops Milieu/Assets/BundledLightRigs.Data.cs`, which links Milieu's own probe
  and estimator. The hand-written `BundledLightRigs.Find` returns an entry only when its `.atex` pin SHA-256 matches and
  its rig is a valid current estimate.
  - Nothing is downloaded, decoded or opened at runtime.
  - Rejected alternatives: a runtime BC7 decode (a new decoder in the game process), the thumbnails (lamps average
    below the clip level), and new release assets (a tag for about 5 KB).
- **Versioning rule:** any change to an estimator constant or algorithm means bumping `LdrLightEstimator.Version` and
  regenerating the table; the same applies to `LightExtractor.Version`.
  - The goldens, the committed-JPEG hash test and the Slow regeneration test fail with that instruction.
  - The brightness constants need neither.
  - Version 2 was taken for the revised clip rule before anything shipped.
- **Panel:** the "lights on" statuses read "Estimated from the image: …". An estimated note (spot directions at a fixed
  distance, lamps under about 0.25° missed) shows while lighting is on. `NeedsHdr` became `NoLightInfo` (an unknown
  kind or no rig).
- **Default stays on** (ND-1): every default-config user in GPose now gets lights for any image. There is no new native
  code, and ADR 0009's mitigations apply as they are.

## Known limits

- Lights sit in each spot's direction at a fixed distance, not at the lamp's real position (panoramas carry no depth).
- A lamp needs a clipped core about 0.25° across (about 6 pixels at 8K, 3 at 4K).
- A lamp is dropped when it is under 5% of the largest spot's area.
- In images with more than 8 spots, a lamp must be at least 3× the median spot (with the fallback above).
- Deep-orange or red cores, and warm cores with G under 250, are not clipped.
- Small cream or sunlit surfaces can become false lamps.
- Bundled scenes, as estimated:
  - **Sunset is fills only.** The committed `sunset.jpeg`'s sun core was softened by its preparation (R ≥ 250 but G
    240–249 and B 100–180), so nothing clips. The original source does get its key.
  - **Forest:** the sun behind the trunks isn't clipped, so bright fog gaps near the horizon become the key and lamps.
  - **Warehouse:** one false lamp on a sunlit wall patch.
- Follow-up: hand-tuned rig overrides for Sunset and Forest.

## Consequences

Easier: every image and bundled scene lights the character with no setup; the estimate is pure, unit-tested on
synthetic panoramas, cached, and the bundled rigs cost no download or decode.
Harder: brightness is an assumption to tune in game; the geometry constants are tied to the bundled table (bump and
regenerate); the clip rule trades missed coloured cores against false lamps on bright surfaces.
